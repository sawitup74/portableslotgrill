Fishing and Boat Day Shore Cooking

Taking a portable charcoal grill on a fishing or boat day: carrying it as cargo and cooking only on shore.

A flat-pack grill stows easily aboard. Slot Grill's FAQ is clear about the rule: carry it as cargo, cook on shore (a sandbar or beach) on a stable non-combustible surface where fires are permitted, and never light a charcoal grill on a boat deck or near the boat.

Shore setup

  • Sand or gravel above the waterline.
  • Away from the boat, fuel and vegetation.
  • Bucket of water for ash.

Cooking your catch

Follow local regulations. Keep fish on ice until cooking; cook to 145°F or until opaque and flaky.

Away-from-home ground rules

  • Ask first. Venue, park, campground, beach, RV park and building rules decide whether charcoal is allowed, and where.
  • Check for burn bans on dry, windy days.
  • Outdoors only. Never under an awning, in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin: charcoal makes carbon monoxide.
  • Non-combustible ground with clearance from vehicles, tents, dunes, brush and people.
  • Never unattended; ash goes in a metal can when cold.

Two-minute setup anywhere

  1. Find level, non-combustible ground.
  2. Assemble: end piece (text out), two-tab side panel, second end piece, then lock in the V with the other side panel. Check every tab.
  3. Light a chimney nearby on the ground or right in the firebox.
  4. Pour coals into a two-zone layout; set the grates or griddle.
  5. Preheat, scrape, oil, cook.

Slot Grill says setup takes about 30 to 60 seconds once you've done it a couple of times.

Pack-up routine

  1. Let the coals burn down; never leave them unattended.
  2. When completely cold, ash into a lidded metal can or the site's bin.
  3. Scrape grates, wipe cooking surfaces with a thin coat of oil or Seasoning Balm.
  4. Lift grates, slide out the V side panel, pull the end pieces, stack.
  5. Bag it so soot stays off your gear.

Food safety without a kitchen

  • Cooler at 40°F or below; raw meat sealed at the bottom.
  • Separate tongs and trays for raw and cooked.
  • Perishables out no more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F).
  • Soap and water for hands; a thermometer for doneness.

Questions people ask

Can I grill on my boat?

Not with a charcoal grill. Carry it as cargo and cook on shore where fires are permitted.
